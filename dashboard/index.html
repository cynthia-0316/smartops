Predictive maintenance decision support

Decide which engines to inspect first, when you cannot inspect them all.

Enter six sensor readings or upload a fleet file. SmartOps estimates how likely each engine is to carry the dataset's Needs Maintenance label and ranks them, so planners can spend limited inspection time where it counts. It supports a human decision. It does not make repair decisions.

Check one engine Prioritize a fleet

How it works

  1. InputSix sensor readings per engine: RPM, three pressures, two temperatures.
  2. Model outputA risk score between 0 and 1, from a model validated on held-out data.
  3. InterpretationHow the score compares with the development fleet and which sensors push it up or down.
  4. DecisionCompare the score with a threshold you set. Above it, an engine is recommended for human inspection.
  5. ActionExport a ranked inspection list and hand it to the workshop.

Recommendation summary

How a company would use SmartOps

An illustrative scenario for a fleet or plant maintenance planner. It is not a deployed system, and it is not based on a real company.

1. Weekly routine

  • Export the latest reading for each engine into a CSV (six sensors).
  • Upload it on Fleet Priorities. Enter this week's inspection capacity.
  • Inspect from the top of the ranked list down. Export the list for the workshop.
  • Check any engine with an out-of-range warning first. The sensor may be faulty.

2. Is the model worth using?

  • On Business Impact, enter your own costs and compare three options: inspect every engine, inspect a random sample of the same size, or inspect the highest-risk engines first.
  • If a missed case costs about the same as an unnecessary inspection, ranking by risk lowers expected cost. If a miss costs about 2.5 times more, inspecting everything is about as cheap.
  • The costs shown are assumptions. Replace them with real figures.

3. If sensors were connected live

  • Assumption, not built: this prototype reads a file you upload. It has no connection to live sensors.
  • A live feed could send each engine's latest six readings to the same model on a schedule (for example once a day) and refresh the ranked list.
  • It would need the same sensors and units as the training data, a rule for faulty or missing readings, and a place to record each decision.
  • The model sees one snapshot per engine. It does not track trends over time.

Business recommendation

For the maintenance manager. Based on this dataset and on assumed costs. It is not validated on a real fleet.

Use SmartOps when

  • You cannot inspect every engine in the period.
  • A missed case costs clearly more than an unnecessary inspection, but not so much that inspecting everything is cheaper.
  • A planner will review the ranked list. The score decides the order of inspection, not whether repair is needed.

Do not rely on it when

  • A missed case costs about 2.5 times an unnecessary inspection or more. Inspecting every engine is then about as cheap.
  • Readings come from other engine types or sensors than the training data, or a reading is outside the training range.
  • You need to know which part is faulty. The model cannot say.

Before adopting it

  • Re-check the model on your own fleet data and your own definition of "needs maintenance".
  • Replace the assumed costs with your real inspection and missed-case costs.
  • Start with a small pilot. Record each inspection result and compare it with the score every month.
  • Keep routine inspections for engines below the threshold.

    Model insights

    The model is additive: each sensor adds or subtracts risk independently, and the six effects are summed. That makes it possible to show exactly how every sensor moves the score. These patterns describe the model and this dataset. They are not proven physical causes of engine failure.

    Global: which sensors matter most

    Average size of each sensor's effect on the log-odds across the 16,604 development engines (larger = the score reacts more to that sensor). This describes the model overall, not any one engine.

    Global: response curve

    Are the probabilities trustworthy?

    Development engines grouped by predicted risk (out-of-fold). If the model is well calibrated, the observed share of Needs Maintenance labels matches the predicted share.

    Local vs global explanations

    Global (this page) summarizes how the model behaves across all engines.

    Local (Predict page) shows how each sensor reading moved one engine's score relative to that sensor's average effect in the development data. Because the model is additive, those effects add up exactly to the final log-odds, so no approximation (such as SHAP) is needed.

    Responsible AI

    SmartOps is an inspection-prioritization aid, not an autonomous industrial safety system. A person must decide what to inspect and what to repair.

    Model limitations

    • Signal is modest: held-out test ROC AUC is …, so given one engine with the label and one without, the model scores the labelled one higher about … of the time.
    • Many different model types reach the same score, which suggests the limit is the information in the six sensors, not the algorithm.
    • No time or engine identifier: trends, remaining life, and per-engine history are not modelled.
    • The label is binary and cannot explain why an engine needs maintenance.

    Data provenance

    • Public Hugging Face dataset, MIT license per its card. The card gives no original source.
    • It is unknown whether the data are real or synthetic, how they were collected, and how the label was assigned.
    • Results show the method works on this dataset. They are not real-world validation on a fleet.
    • If rows from one engine appear in both training and test data, every score could be optimistic. This cannot be checked without engine IDs.

    Risk of false negatives

    • A missed case (false negative) can mean an engine that needed attention was not inspected. Lowering the threshold catches more, at the price of more unnecessary inspections.
    • Engines below the threshold are not certified healthy. Keep routine inspection schedules.
    • Never use a low score to override an alarm, a driver report, or a safety procedure.

    Human oversight

    • The output is a ranked list for a planner. The planner decides what to inspect.
    • Require human review before any maintenance work order, and for every engine with an out-of-range input warning.
    • Record the threshold and capacity used for each decision so the choice can be audited.

    Sensor errors and distribution shift

    • Faulty sensors produce misleading scores. Inputs outside the training range trigger a warning, and the model only extrapolates linearly there.
    • Other engine types, operating conditions, or sensor calibrations can shift the data. Re-validate before using the model on a new fleet.
    • Thresholds here are business settings. They are not engineering safety boundaries.

    Privacy and security

    • The dataset has no personal or engine identifiers. Uploaded files are processed in your browser and are not sent to a server by this dashboard.
    • Uploads can still contain sensitive fleet data. Remove identifiers you do not need and follow your organization's data rules.
    • No credentials or tokens are stored in this application.

    Suggested monitoring after deployment

    • Compare predicted risk with confirmed maintenance findings each month (calibration drift).
    • Track precision and recall of flagged engines against the capacity used.
    • Track the distribution of each sensor against the training ranges shown on Model Insights.
    • Review false negatives individually and retrain only through the development-data protocol, keeping a held-out test set.
    • Replace the assumed costs with real inspection and failure costs before relying on any savings figure.
